## Intentionally rejected behaviors
- novelty or popularity as evidence of value/significance;
- product launch as automatic technology origin;
- single-inventor stories without evidence;
- historical benchmarks as proof of application performance;
- tool use as proof of organizational permission;
- fluency as proof of judgment;
- retrieval as proof of correctness/applicability;
- technical capability as proof of readiness;
- generic “human oversight” advice without contextual reasoning;
- identical recommendations across materially different contexts without justification;
- rewriting history to fit organizational context;
- invented organizational facts;
- unsupported causal stories;
- vague “monitor developments” triggers.

## Team invariant
Historical evidence determines what the technology inherited.
Application analysis determines where those inheritances matter.
Organizational context determines their relevance, consequence and acceptable authority.
Empirical evidence—not historical novelty—determines whether those capabilities create organizational value.
